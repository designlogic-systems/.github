# DesignLogic Writing Standard

Version: v0.1  
Status: Active public writing guidance  
Scope: Public DesignLogic writing and narrative surfaces

DesignLogic writing should follow the same discipline the company is building toward:

```text
AI-assisted work should preserve meaning, not just produce fluent output.
````

That principle applies to the writing itself.

If DesignLogic says meaning, source, boundary, review, and handoff matter, then its public writing cannot sound like a generic AI-generated template. The writing has to show that someone actually understood the failure pattern and wrote from the pressure point.

Good DesignLogic writing should hold meaning, not just sound clean.

---

## Scope Note

This standard applies most strongly to public narrative surfaces:

* repo front doors
* README introductions
* About pages
* LinkedIn posts
* articles
* public explanations
* landing-page copy
* public product framing

It applies more lightly to governance, reference, status, schema, and boundary artifacts.

Those files may need deliberate repetition, status blocks, exact terminology, and repeated authority boundaries for auditability. Do not remove necessary review posture, maturity posture, validation posture, or authority-boundary language just to make prose sound more natural.

Some repetition is bad writing.

Some repetition is governance.

Know the difference.

---

## Core Principle

DesignLogic public writing must preserve:

* the real claim
* the source meaning
* the operating boundary
* the maturity posture
* the unresolved uncertainty
* the specific failure pattern being addressed

The writing should not flatten DesignLogic into generic AI-company language.

The reader should feel that someone has seen the failure pattern clearly enough to name it without over-selling it.

---

## Writing Posture

DesignLogic should sound:

* clear, but not sterile
* intelligent, but not inflated
* grounded, but not dull
* precise, but not mechanical
* confident, but not overclaimed
* human, but not careless

The goal is not “friendly startup copy.”

The goal is serious human clarity.

A skeptical builder, founder, operator, reviewer, or technical reader should be able to tell that the writing came from the actual problem, not from a marketing frame.

---

## The Main Enemy: Visible AI Scaffolding

Bad AI writing often exposes its structure too cleanly.

It can sound organized while still feeling empty.

Avoid patterns like:

* “A this becomes a that.”
* “The answer may... The summary may... The workflow may...”
* “Not just X, but Y.”
* “In today’s rapidly evolving AI landscape...”
* “At the intersection of...”
* “This matters because...”
* “The goal is not only to X, but to Y.”
* neat three-part sequences
* repeated sentence openings
* overly balanced paragraph rhythm
* generic contrast pairs
* slogan endings that feel preassembled
* abstract noun stacks such as “governance, transparency, accountability, innovation, transformation”

These patterns are not always wrong in isolation.

The problem is accumulation.

Once the reader senses the template, trust drops.

---

## Preferred Movement

Do not draft from generic marketing sections.

Avoid writing from this shape:

```text
problem
-> solution
-> product
-> differentiator
-> mission
```

That structure is too visible.

Draft from meaning blocks instead:

1. What pressure or failure is DesignLogic responding to?
2. Where does the failure actually happen?
3. What do people usually miss?
4. What does DesignLogic preserve or make visible?
5. What boundary should not be crossed?
6. What does the product, framework, or artifact help organize?
7. What should the reader understand differently after reading?

Writing should move by thought, not by template.

---

## Sentence Rhythm

DesignLogic writing should feel human enough to carry pressure.

That means:

* vary sentence length
* avoid uniform paragraph shapes
* mix direct claims with slower explanation
* let some short lines stand alone
* do not over-smooth every transition
* allow complexity where the thought needs it
* avoid making every paragraph feel equally polished

Bad rhythm:

```text
AI outputs can look complete. AI outputs can lose context. AI outputs can create risk.
```

Better:

```text
The output may look complete enough to keep moving. That is often the danger. Once the rough edges are gone, the reviewer may stop looking for the signals that disappeared with them.
```

The second version has pressure. It has specificity. It moves like someone is thinking.

## DesignLogic Language

Use language that fits the actual lane.

Strong terms:

* meaning
* source
* boundary
* intent
* review
* handoff
* definition
* trace
* constraint
* execution
* structure
* continuity
* validation surface
* builder-ready artifact
* semantic backend infrastructure

Use carefully:

* governance
* accountability
* AI workflows
* product intelligence
* automation
* infrastructure
* semantic systems

Avoid unless strongly justified:

* revolutionary
* next-generation
* cutting-edge
* seamless
* unlock
* transform the future
* autonomous reasoning
* self-correcting AI
* enterprise-grade
* ethical AI at scale
* AI-powered innovation

DesignLogic should not sound like it is selling “smarter AI.”

It should sound like it is building the missing structure between AI output and dependable downstream use.

---

## Boundary And Maturity Language

Do not make the writing sound more mature than the work allows.

Preserve boundary language where it matters:

```text
handoff is not deployment
trace is not proof
review is not authority
builder-ready does not mean build-authorized
example is not implementation proof
proof-slice plan is not proof-slice completion
```

These statements may look repetitive.

They are supposed to.

In DesignLogic reference and governance artifacts, boundary repetition protects meaning. Do not polish it away.

In public narrative prose, use boundary language where it carries the claim, not as filler.

---

## Reader Test

Before publishing, ask:

```text
Would a skeptical founder, builder, operator, or product person believe someone actually thought this through?
```

Then ask the harder DesignLogic question:

```text
Does this writing preserve meaning, or does it merely sound coherent?
```

If the writing sounds polished but interchangeable, it fails.

If it could appear on almost any AI startup page, it fails.

If the reader can feel the specific DesignLogic problem underneath the prose, it is closer.

---

## Anti-Pattern Pass

Every public-facing DesignLogic draft should go through this pass before review.

Check for:

1. repeated sentence openings
2. parallel list scaffolding
3. “this becomes that” sequences
4. “not just X but Y” framing
5. generic LinkedIn setup language
6. abstract claims with no concrete operational anchor
7. too many clean transitions
8. too many balanced paragraphs
9. inflated product maturity claims
10. language that sounds like AI describing AI

Then revise for:

* more concrete nouns
* more natural sentence variation
* fewer symmetrical examples
* clearer source of the claim
* stronger operational grounding
* less slogan shape
* more evidence of thought

---

## Product Philosophy Constraint

DesignLogic writing must not contradict DesignLogic philosophy.

DesignLogic is about preserving meaning before AI-assisted work becomes operational.

So the writing should preserve meaning too.

That means:

* do not flatten the company into generic AI language
* do not let polish erase the actual claim
* do not make the writing sound more mature than the product boundary allows
* do not hide uncertainty or product status behind broad claims
* do not make the prose so smooth that it stops feeling accountable

The writing should carry the same discipline the system is meant to support.

---

## Assistant Instruction

When using an AI writing assistant for DesignLogic public content, use this instruction:

```text
Write this in the DesignLogic style.

Use human, high-variation prose. Do not use generic AI-writing scaffolds, repeated sentence openings, neat three-part sequences, “A this / A that” patterns, “this becomes that” patterns, “not just X but Y,” or LinkedIn-safe slogan structure.

Draft from meaning blocks, not marketing sections.

Preserve the real claim, the source meaning, the boundary, and the product maturity posture.

Before showing the final version, run an anti-AI-writing pass and remove anything that sounds templated, overly symmetrical, inflated, or interchangeable with another AI company.

DesignLogic writing must demonstrate the company thesis: meaning has to survive AI-assisted transformation.
```

---

## Final Publishing Standard

A DesignLogic piece is ready when it does four things:

1. Names a real failure pattern.
2. Explains it in language a serious builder or operator can recognize.
3. Shows why DesignLogic exists in that gap.
4. Sounds like it was written by someone thinking, not by a system filling a frame.

The writing does not need to be flashy.

It needs to hold.
